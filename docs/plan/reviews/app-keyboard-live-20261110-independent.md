The geometry arithmetic is correct: frame `[0,96][1440,2960]` has height 2864px; content/visible top inset 2863 leaves a **1px declared bottom band**, whose screen top is about y2959. This is the region implied to WindowManager by the reported insets, not an independent measurement of the input view's actual height or painted pixels. In API28's reference implementation, `InputMethodService.Insets.contentTopInsets` controls the space needed to resize/pan the underlying app, while `visibleTopInsets` describes visible obstruction for panning. Neither is a pixel clipping rectangle. The default `onComputeInsets` can use decor height when the input frame is not visible. Samsung/WeType can override these values; the captured touchable mode is 3 (custom region), so the AOSP default is a semantic reference, not proof of WeType's implementation.

Reference: [AOSP Android 9 InputMethodService](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/android-9.0.0_r1/core/java/android/inputmethodservice/InputMethodService.java). Bounded relevant declarations/method excerpts, source SHA and line numbers are retained in the independent raw file.

The records support current app focus, a live served WebView/InputConnection, a bound WeType process and positive show/start/service flags at the times sampled. They exclude a simple claim that this snapshot has no focused editor or no active connection. They **do not** prove successful visible keyboard rendering: `mInputShown`, `mIsInputViewShown` and `HAS_DRAWN` are state/surface flags. They also do not exclude a hidden/collapsed input area with stale or inconsistent show flags, a custom IME inset declaration, or a prior app/system lifecycle trigger. The different dumps span approximately 09:41–09:46, rather than being one atomic observation. Repeated near-empty insets and positive flags support a sustained mismatch, but not a complete intervening event history or the responsible component.

Its large gray mask covers much of the region in which an IME could otherwise appear, so this reviewer cannot independently establish absence of keyboard pixels from that redacted image alone. The author's original-image observation is retained as author evidence, not promoted to this reviewer's own visual finding. UIA supplied no parsable hierarchy and cannot strengthen this conclusion.

The parent's already authorized single controlled hide/show can test whether show-state, insets and actual keys change together; a successful recovery would establish that transition's effect, not a permanent product fix.
